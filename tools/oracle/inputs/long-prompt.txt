A wide photograph of a surfer at dawn on a cold Atlantic beach in Brittany, taken from the top of a granite cliff covered in gorse and heather, looking down at a long crescent of pale sand between two rocky headlands. The tide is halfway out and the wet sand reflects the sky like a mirror, pink near the horizon and steel blue above it. A young woman in a black full wetsuit with a hood pulled back walks toward the water, carrying a worn yellow longboard under her right arm; her hair is dark, wet and tied in a low knot, and her bare feet leave a line of prints that the foam is already erasing behind her. She is small in the frame, placed on the lower third, slightly left of center, and she casts a long thin shadow across the sand toward the cliff.

The sea is the subject as much as she is. Three sets of waves come in from the west in regular lines, each one rising dark green and translucent before it breaks; the light from the low sun passes through the lip of the nearest wave and turns it the color of bottle glass. The wave breaks from right to left, peeling cleanly along a sandbar, and the white water rolls forward in a wide band of foam with small rainbow spray where the offshore wind lifts the crest. Further out, a second surfer sits on her board beyond the break, a black silhouette waiting, and a cormorant flies low over the water between them.

The headland on the left is made of dark slate and pink granite, with deep vertical cracks, patches of orange lichen, and a ruined stone watchtower on its top; waves explode against its base and throw white spray twenty meters into the air. On the right, the cliff slopes down to a small car park where an old white van with rust on its wheel arches is parked, its back doors open, a towel and a thermos on the bumper. A wooden footpath with a rope handrail zigzags down from the car park to the beach, and a faded sign at the top shows a tide table.

The sky is clear except for a band of thin high clouds lit from below, and the moon is still visible, pale and almost full, above the right headland. The air looks cold and clean; there is a faint mist at the waterline where the spray hangs in the light. Everything is sharp from the foreground heather to the horizon, with the fine texture of sand ripples, individual pebbles, strands of kelp washed up in a dark line along the high tide mark, and the small bubbles in the foam visible at full size.

Shot on a medium format camera with a 45 mm lens at f/8, natural light only, no filters, true colors, gentle contrast, the highlights of the foam kept intact and the shadows of the cliff open and full of detail. The mood is quiet and early, the beach empty except for the two surfers, the only sounds the wind and the surf. No text, no logo, no watermark, no frame, no people other than the two surfers, no boats, no buildings other than the tower and the van. Realistic proportions, a natural pose mid-stride, the board held at a slight angle, the leash coiled around her ankle, a few drops of water on her face catching the light, a calm and focused expression as she looks at the waves.

Behind the first cliff, the coast continues north in a line of further headlands that fade from dark blue to pale grey with distance, each one softer than the last, and a lighthouse painted in black and white stripes stands on the farthest point, its lamp still turning faintly in the morning light. Above the beach, a path of crushed shells runs along the edge of the dunes, lined with marram grass bent by the wind, sea holly with blue thistle heads, and small yellow flowers; a pair of oystercatchers with orange beaks stand on a rock at the edge of the water, and a line of gulls rests on the sand facing the wind.
